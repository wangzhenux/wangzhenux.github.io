<section id="s01">
  <Lede>
    Twilio Regions lets users deploy apps in specific geographic regions —
    solving challenges for customers doing business outside the US, including
    data sovereignty, performance, and disaster recovery.
  </Lede>

  <BodyText>
    But customers often don't know about the different Twilio regions and their
    benefits, so they build products in the default region (US1) even when it
    isn't the best choice for data sovereignty or performance. The product was
    capable. The onboarding wasn't.
  </BodyText>

  <SectionH>Why low adoption?</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    These are the top three pain points causing low adoption, based on the past
    research.
  </BodyText>

  <CardRow>
    <PainCard num="01" title="Lack of visibility">
      Twilio Regions is a relatively new feature, and many users are not aware
      of its benefits or how to use it.
    </PainCard>
    <PainCard num="02" title="Limited use cases">
      Users need to evaluate whether their use case justifies the use of Twilio
      Regions with limited features.
    </PainCard>
    <PainCard num="03" title="Technical complexity">
      Setting up Twilio Regions can be technically complex, as users need to
      re-route traffic in their application.
    </PainCard>
  </CardRow>

  <SplitFeature image={{ src: "/cases/twilio/jtbd.png", alt: "Job-to-be-done statement for the Regional Onboarding project", width: 1486, height: 1166 }}>
    <Jtbd
      role="Twilio customer interested in developing an application for non-US customers"
      want="be informed of the existence of different Twilio regions"
      outcome="decide if building in a non-US1 region makes sense for my use case"
    />
  </SplitFeature>
</section>

<section id="s04">
  <SectionH>Validating with usability testing</SectionH>

  <ResearchStats cols={2}>
    <Stat num="10" label="Participants">
      <span class="item">4 current, international Twilio customers</span>
      <span class="item">6 international non-customers</span>
    </Stat>
    <Stat num="2" label="Objectives">
      <span class="item">Evaluate regional onboarding and configuration concepts</span>
      <span class="item">Determine what features would make Default Region useful for customers</span>
    </Stat>
  </ResearchStats>

  <SectionH small>Key findings</SectionH>
  <QuoteRow>
    <QuoteCard
      label="Finding 01"
      heading="Complex onboarding"
      quote="Choosing new regions might be complicated — I don't even know if someone needs to choose a default location."
    />
    <QuoteCard
      label="Finding 02"
      heading="Default Region misleading"
      quote="Setting the Default Region will impact the language [display] and billing [i.e., what currency I'm billed in]."
    />
    <QuoteCard
      label="Finding 03"
      heading="Confusing routing concept"
      quote="Do I need to have the same setup in the US and Australia?"
    />
  </QuoteRow>

  <SectionH small>Recommendations</SectionH>
  <CardRow>
    <PainCard num="01" title="Further research">
      Partner with Growth and CX to continue research into the customer's
      experience between the Evaluate and Acquire phases.
    </PainCard>
    <PainCard num="02" title="Re-evaluate Default Region">
      Consider removing the Default Region concept from the Console and its
      functionalities.
    </PainCard>
    <PainCard num="03" title="Phased approach">
      Phase Regions into the Console and evaluate customer response at each stage
      of maturity.
    </PainCard>
  </CardRow>
</section>

<section id="s05">
  <SectionH>A phased approach</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    Based on the research and the discussion with relevant stakeholders, I came
    up with a **phased approach to strategically onboard customers with Twilio
    Regions**.
  </BodyText>

  <Phase
    num="01"
    label="Phase 1 · Shipped"
    title={"“👋 Ahoy, we have Twilio Regions for you”"}

    Increase exposure of Twilio Regions and educate on its benefits without
    disrupting the current customer experience — a guided tour that pins the
    region based on location, plus regional docs added to the last step of the
    self-serve flow.

<section id="s07">
  <SectionH>What I learned</SectionH>
  <Reflection>
    <ReflectionCard label="On decision making" heading="RAPID decision-making framework">
      Use the RAPID decision-making framework to identify the key roles —
      Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, and Decide — and the associated
      deliverables at the beginning of the project.
    </ReflectionCard>
    <ReflectionCard label="On strategy" heading="Strategic, phased thinking">
      It's not always possible to achieve a design all at once, especially when
      there are numerous dependencies and constraints. Consider taking a phased
      approach that gradually works toward the north star.
    </ReflectionCard>
  </Reflection>
