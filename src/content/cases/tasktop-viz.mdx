<section id="s02">
  <SectionH>Background</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    In 2018, Tasktop CEO Dr. Mik Kersten wrote a market-developing book called
    *Project to Product* — I assisted the CEO with designing the interior
    graphics and the Flow Framework™ diagram. It introduced how organizations
    move from a project-oriented to a product-oriented model to survive and
    thrive in the age of digital disruption, using the Flow Framework™ as a
    blueprint.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    Tasktop began developing the first purpose-built tool to implement the Flow
    Framework™ — one that could automatically extract the end-to-end tool data
    underpinning software delivery, and translate that data into a common
    language that business leaders could understand.
  </BodyText>

  <Figure
    src="/cases/tasktop-viz/p2p.png"
    alt="Project to Product book and the Flow Framework™"
    caption="Project to Product + the Flow Framework™."
  />
</section>

<section id="s03">
  <SectionH>Challenges</SectionH>
  <CardRow>
    <PainCard num="01" title="From zero with limited time">
      This was a totally new product with few existing competitors to learn from,
      and time and resources were limited.
    </PainCard>
    <PainCard num="02" title="Vague customer needs">
      We knew customers needed the tool, but we didn't have details — and
      customers weren't clear about what to expect either.
    </PainCard>
    <PainCard num="03" title="Difficult to test">
      It was difficult to find prospective users with the domain-specific
      knowledge to understand the concepts.
    </PainCard>
  </CardRow>
  <BodyText>
    On top of that, there were a lot of unknowns: technical difficulties and what
    data was available remained unknown, and there was no clear roadmap.
  </BodyText>
</section>

<section id="s04">
  <SectionH>Features and early experiments</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    Before starting, we ran a **discovery workshop** with stakeholders —
    including the CEO, VP of Product, and product managers — to create alignment
    on overall goals, scope, and milestones, and to gather user needs. Out of
    that came the feature list: users should be able to connect to different
    tools, see the artifact types and projects once connected, intuitively
    understand the flow metrics, add and customize products, categorize artifact
    types and states into flow items and flow states, change configuration at any
    time and see the results, see all five flow metrics, and analyze those
    metrics to get insights.
  </BodyText>

  <SectionH small>Early data analysis and experiment</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    The new product is very data-centric. To generate and visualize metrics, we
    first needed to validate what data we could get and, from that, what
    visualizations would make sense to users. We worked as a team with an agency
    that had expertise in both design and data visualization, over three months.
    During the collaboration, **I touched base with them almost every week to
    keep track of the process and make sure the visualizations met the
    requirements.** We analyzed the data and brainstormed a range of ideas
    together — from the Sankey diagram to a creative solar-system visualization.
    To demo the results we found examples and also implemented the idea in React
    using sample data; the playback turned out well, and the stakeholders were
    excited to see it.
  </BodyText>

<section id="s05">
  <SectionH>Narrowing the scope</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    After the early investigations, experiments, and meetings with engineers and
    stakeholders, we decided to narrow the scope to the minimum set of features.
    Moving forward, we advanced the product using three guiding principles.
  </BodyText>

  <CardRow>
    <PainCard num="01" title="Quick time to value">
      Give value instantly if we can.
    </PainCard>
    <PainCard num="02" title="Baby steps">
      Give a little, get a lot; give a little more, get a lot more.
    </PainCard>
    <PainCard num="03" title="Make abstractions easy">
      Modeling must occur at various layers.
    </PainCard>
  </CardRow>

  <BodyText>
    We simplified the initial workflow into a clearer sequence: model the product
    value stream, add artifact types, view the dashboard, model flow states, and
    view flow metrics.
