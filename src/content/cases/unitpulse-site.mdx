<section id="p1" data-reveal>
  <PartHeader num="01" title="Invisible where renters start" />
  <BodyText>
    A renter's search often starts in a chat now, and gets answered before
    anyone clicks. The old UnitPulse site couldn't show up there or in Google:
    it was a client-only single-page app, with nothing server-rendered for a
    search engine or an answer engine to read. The rebuild had to stay rich and
    app-like, make every home a page that could be indexed and quoted, and keep
    the chats themselves out of the index.

    Renters also arrive in different states. Some know their budget,
    neighborhood and move-in date, and want filters and a map. Some know the
    vibe of a place but not the specifics. Some are down to a few homes and want
    answers about one of them: the commute, the schools, the walk.
    {/* Design reasoning, not research (Zhen 2026-10-08): claim no interviews. */}
  </BodyText>

<section id="p2" data-reveal>
  <PartHeader num="02" title="One search, two ways in" />
  <BodyText>
    So I designed the two modes as one. The conversation handles intent, the
    filters handle precision, and both write to a single shared state, so the
    chat, the filter chips and the map can't disagree. To ship in about six
    weeks, I scoped it to one home, from search to contact. Bulk tours, blogs
    and FAQ pages waited.
  </BodyText>
  <ShowcaseVideo
    src="/cases/unitpulse-site/shared-state.mp4"
    poster="/cases/unitpulse-site/shared-state-poster.jpg"
    label="The unified search: typing in the chat, tapping a chip and changing a filter all update one shared state that drives the result list and the live map with price pins"
    width={2160}
    height={1216}
  />
  <BodyText>
    Four smaller decisions make the shared state easy to steer:

    - When a must-have is missing, the assistant shows a short stepped card
      inside the chat, and every step can be skipped.
    - What it understood appears as an editable summary and as removable chips
      in the composer, so dropping "pet friendly" is one tap.
    - Price, Beds, Filters and Sort still sit above the results for people who
      prefer them, and they write to the same state as the chat.
    - After each result set, it suggests the next useful narrowing (budget,
      bedrooms, amenities or move-in date), so a search doesn't dead-end.
  </BodyText>
</section>

  <PartHeader num="04" title="Fair Housing, tested" />
  <BodyText>
    A rental search can't steer renters by protected class, and an AI copilot
    makes that easy to get wrong. I built a regression suite of 47 cases across
    9 intent families, in English and Chinese, each judged on how the message
    was routed as well as on the visible reply. It ran at 34 pass, 9 fail and 4
    blocked, and it caught a real blocker: one path extracted a protected-class
    attribute as a filter.
