<section id="s01">
  <Lede>
    No-shows are expensive. A prospect who books a tour and never arrives burns
    an agent's time and costs the property a chance to lease the unit.
  </Lede>

  <BodyText>
    The day-of reminder flow was the single biggest lever on that loss — CRM-2906
    traced the no-show problem squarely back to it. And the messages worked
    against themselves: there were too many of them, and they ran too long, which
    risked the worst outcome of all — a prospect opting out of texts entirely.
  </BodyText>

  <BodyText>
    So the brief carried a real tension. **Reminders have to cut no-shows without
    annoying prospects into unsubscribing** — and every message has to stay
    A2P 10DLC and TCPA-compliant. Those weren't competing concerns to balance
    later; they were the design constraint from the first message.
  </BodyText>
</section>

<section id="s03">
  <SectionH>Design decisions</SectionH>
  <SectionDeck>Six decisions, each with the reason behind it.</SectionDeck>

  <BodyText>
    **1 · Interactive confirmation, not a passive reminder.** Short, clear
    messages that ask the prospect to *actively confirm* — so the appointment is
    locked in rather than merely announced. An unanswered reminder is easy to
    ignore; a question is harder to leave hanging. (CRM-2906 / CRM-3402:
    "lock in the appointment, reduce no-shows; interactive, short, clear.")
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **2 · A language-agnostic confirm.** A single short reply — for example,
    "1" — confirms the tour regardless of the language the prospect reads in.
    That matters for a bilingual renter base, where a "reply YES" prompt quietly
    excludes anyone not reading in English.
    {/* ‹EST-confirm› lock the real v1 mechanic ("1" vs "YES/confirm"); copy is hedged to read correctly under either */}
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **3 · Right message, right moment.** A booking confirmation on schedule; a
    reminder that carries the confirm ask ahead of the tour; and a clean
    cancellation path when plans change. Each message earns its send.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **4 · Trim to reduce opt-outs.** I cut the number and length of the touring
    messages and removed the feedback request from the confirmation messages, so
    the essential confirm ask isn't buried under things that can wait. Fewer,
    shorter messages are both kinder to the prospect and safer against opt-outs.
    (CRM release notes, December 2024.)
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **5 · Brand-aware templates.** The templates adapt their branding for partner
    and third-party properties that lease under their own brand, so a reminder
    looks like it came from the property the prospect is touring — not from a
    platform they've never heard of. (CRM-2731.)
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **6 · Compliance as a design constraint.** A2P 10DLC and TCPA-compliant
    opt-in and message design were built in from the start, not bolted on — the
    same registration work I'd done on the Twilio side made the rules concrete
    rather than abstract.
  </BodyText>
</section>

<section id="s04">
  <SectionH>Craft detail</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    The three production email templates — the booking confirmation, the reminder
    that carries the confirm CTA, and the cancellation — were built on the **real

<section id="s05">
  <SectionH>The AI tie-in</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    The tour flow later became conversational. Scheduling, rescheduling, and
    cancelling a tour are now handled by AI **"Tour Agents,"** and the prospect's
    confirm reply feeds a signal back into the AI leasing system — so a "yes" is
    no longer just a calendar event, it's input the broader system can act on.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    That connects this lifecycle work to the wider [CRM Copilot ecosystem](/work/crm-copilot):
    the lightweight confirm interaction designed here became one of the everyday
    signals the leasing AI reads.
  </BodyText>
</section>

<section id="s07">
  <SectionH>Reflection</SectionH>
  <Reflection>
    <ReflectionCard label="On measurement" heading="A first signal, not a verdict">
      A 40% relative drop in month one is a strong start, but one month is a
      signal, not proof. What I'd instrument next is the no-show delta by message
      variant, so the win can be attributed to specific copy rather than to the
      redesign as a whole.
    </ReflectionCard>
    <ReflectionCard label="On what's next" heading="Design it as an experiment">
      The natural next moves are tests, not a one-shot fix: confirm-rate by
      channel (SMS vs email), and reminder timing — does a 24-hour reminder beat
      a same-morning one? Each is a question the messaging system can answer if
      it's built to be measured.
    </ReflectionCard>
