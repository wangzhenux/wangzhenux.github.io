<section id="s01">
  <Lede>
    TechScene is an event site dedicated to Austin's technology scene — meetups,
    classes, talks, and networking, gathered in one place.
  </Lede>

  <BodyText>
    Austin already had event sites, but they cast a wide net. We got tired of
    wading through general listings just to find the tech gatherings worth
    showing up to. So we built one central place for Austin's tech events,
    classes, and networking — somewhere to learn, play, and network — and cut
    the clutter that never served students, enthusiasts, or working
    professionals.
  </BodyText>

  <BodyText>
    The path from that itch to a finished design ran through research,
    synthesis, and 5 rounds of iterative design. Here's the whole arc.
  </BodyText>

<section id="s02">
  <SectionH>User research</SectionH>
  <SectionDeck>
    We listened in 4 passes: 2 rounds of interviews, a competitive teardown, and
    a Qualtrics survey.
  </SectionDeck>

  <ResearchStats cols={4}>
    <Stat num="6" label="Interviews">Phase one, ages 22–35</Stat>
    <Stat num="6" label="Sites analyzed">Competitive teardown</Stat>
    <Stat num="4" label="Interviews">Phase two, ages 22–60</Stat>
    <Stat num="13" label="Question survey">Fielded in Qualtrics</Stat>
  </ResearchStats>

  <BodyText>
    In the first phase we sat down with 6 people, ages 22 to 35. We asked how
    they experienced tech events, how they searched for them today, and what
    they'd want from a site built just for tech.
  </BodyText>

  <BodyText>
    Next we looked at 6 sites our interviewees and team kept naming — among them
    Meetup, Eventbrite, Do512, SXSW, and Technical.ly. For each, we weighed the
    design, the search, and how events were categorized, noting what worked and
    what didn't, then kept and improved the pieces worth carrying over.
  </BodyText>

  <ImageShowcase>
    <ShowcaseImage
      src="/cases/techscene/competitive_analysis.png"
      alt="Competitive analysis of 6 existing event sites, comparing design, search, and event categorization."
      width={1994}
      height={656}
    />
  </ImageShowcase>

  <BodyText>
    Round one skewed female and student-heavy, so for the second phase we
    recruited 4 people, ages 22 to 60, to balance it out — an instructor, a
    professor, and 2 people working in tech.
  </BodyText>

  <QuoteRow>
    <QuoteCard
      label="University student"
      heading="Wants to go with friends"
      quote="What I care about is when it's happening and how I'll get there. And I want to go with friends — before I commit, I ask if anyone else wants to come."

    <QuoteCard
      label="University professor"
      heading="Bridging school and industry"

    />
    <QuoteCard
      label="Works in tech"
      heading="Relevance and calendar sync"
      quote="I want events tied to my work — who's speaking, what the topic is, how it's run, and who's hosting. And I want to sync it straight into my own calendar."
    />
  </QuoteRow>

  <BodyText>
    Last, we ran a 13-question survey in Qualtrics and shared it through the
    iSchool's Insider newsletter and our own contacts in the tech industry. It
    asked about demographics, how much experience people had with tech events,
    and what they wanted a site like this to hold.
  </BodyText>

<section id="s03">
  <SectionH>Concept & IA</SectionH>

  <BodyText>
    We sorted what we'd heard with an affinity diagram. Each of us read back
    through the interviews and survey, wrote one sticky per candidate area of the
    site, and grouped them our own way — then we merged the groupings into a
    shared map.
  </BodyText>

  <ImageShowcase>
    <ShowcaseImage

<section id="s04">
  <SectionH>Iterative design</SectionH>
  <SectionDeck>5 rounds, paper to hi-fi, tested at every step.</SectionDeck>

  <BodyText>
    We set the blueprint, then started rough — paper prototypes of the main flow.
    Across 5 rounds, from paper sketch to hi-fi, we tested each version and
    refined it, settling the content mapping and controlled vocabulary alongside
    the visual design.
  </BodyText>

  <BodyText>
    We built the wireframes in Balsamiq and Axure — homepage, search results,
    event detail, account, and the create-event flow. Both were quick to
    prototype, which let us test the information architecture before we committed
    to it.
  </BodyText>

  <SectionH small>Usability testing</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    We ran usability testing on every prototype — 15 participants across 25
    sessions in total. Each round's feedback pointed us at the pages that
    mattered most to the experience, and that's where the next round's changes
    went.
  </BodyText>
