<section id="s01">
  <Lede>
    Agents lived inside a deep, menu-driven CRM. Routine actions meant long
    click-paths — and when a function was buried, they pinged tech support just
    to ask whether it was even possible.
  </Lede>

  <Pullquote>
    It takes fifteen clicks through confusing menus just to log that I've
    completed a tour. I wish I could just type it.
    <cite>— David Chen, leasing agent · PRD persona</cite>
  </Pullquote>

  <BodyText>
    Meanwhile the company was shipping a string of AI bets — a Listing Bot, a
    Leasing Bot, a multi-channel inbox — with no unified place to command them.
    The biggest AI investments were at risk of low adoption for want of a front
    door.
  </BodyText>

  <BodyText>
    **The stakes were explicit.** The copilot was scoped as a keystone of the
    2025 AI strategy, a company **P0**, and the cheapest path to a future mobile
    CRM.
  </BodyText>
</section>

<section id="s03">
  <SectionH>Approach</SectionH>
  <SectionDeck>Four principles, against one hard constraint.</SectionDeck>
  <BodyText>
    The constraint set the bar: **a conversational layer only wins if it's faster
    than muscle-memory menus.** Everything below serves that one test.
  </BodyText>

  <BodyText>
    **1 · Conversational-first, typo-forgiving.** Plain commands — however
    loosely typed — map to real CRM actions, not search results.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **2 · Beat the menu, or it dies.** The command commits instantly on send; a
    "working on it" state plus command shortcuts mask LLM latency so the copilot
    *feels* faster than the click-path it replaces.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **3 · Fail gracefully.** Ambiguity earns a clarifying question or a
    suggestion — never a dead-end error.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **4 · Teach by doing.** Relevant commands surface in context (progressive
    discoverability), replacing the old "is this even possible?" trip to support.
  </BodyText>
</section>

<section id="s04">
  <SectionH>The generative-UI container</SectionH>
  <BodyText>
    Rather than hand-design a fixed panel for every command, I designed **one
    adaptive container** that renders model-generated UI — dashboards, forms,
    tables — on demand. New AI capabilities can appear without new screens, and
    the same framework extends to mobile.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    Context is **isolated per browser tab**, so state from one open tab never
    bleeds into another.
  </BodyText>

<section id="s06">
  <SectionH>Strategic considerations</SectionH>
  <SectionDeck>Three decisions, each with the tradeoff it carried.</SectionDeck>

  <BodyText>
    **1 · Perceived speed over feature breadth.** I scoped V1 to the
    highest-frequency commands and over-invested in feedback states.
    **Tradeoff —** fewer commands, in exchange for faster trust.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **2 · A container, not a catalog of screens.** A harder system to build, but
    durable leverage: it scales to a growing AI suite and to mobile without a
    redesign. **Tradeoff —** more upfront systems work for far less per-feature
    design later.
  </BodyText>
  <BodyText>
    **3 · Grounded the command set in real tech-support tickets.** The first
    commands were the frictions that demonstrably existed, not guesses.
    **Tradeoff —** narrower at launch, but every command earned its place.
  </BodyText>
</section>

<section id="s08">
  <SectionH>Reflection</SectionH>
  <Reflection>
    <ReflectionCard label="On leverage" heading="Interface as leverage">
      Designing the system — the container — rather than the screens turned one
      lightweight layer into the front-end for an entire AI roadmap. The cheapest
      surface became the most strategic one.
    </ReflectionCard>
    <ReflectionCard label="On speed" heading="Perceived speed is a design material">
      Against muscle-memory menus, the win wasn't raw model latency — it was the
      feedback choreography that made the copilot feel instant. Designing the wait
      mattered as much as designing the answer.
    </ReflectionCard>
  </Reflection>
</section>

<section id="s09">
  <SectionH>The AI suite behind it</SectionH>
  <SectionDeck>Features I led design on — the bots the command layer fronts.</SectionDeck>

  <SplitFeature image={{ src: "/cases/crm-copilot/listing.svg", alt: "Placeholder for the AI Listing Bot composer / review screen", width: 1600, height: 900 }}>
    <SectionH small>AI Listing Bot</SectionH>
    <BodyText>
      An AI marketing copilot in the same CRM sidebar that automates the listing
      lifecycle: it ingests property data, generates ad copy, picks and overlays
      photos, and publishes rich-media listings across Facebook, TikTok, YouTube,
      Rednote, and local sites — with the agent reviewing and editing before
      anything goes live. I was prototype UI/UX design lead (named on the PRD).
      The principle was **control and transparency** — the agent always sees what
      the AI generated and what it will do before it acts. Status: MVP completed
      Q3 2025, end-to-end from data to automated listing to UTM lead tracking.
      Targeted >90% weekly active usage and a 30% blended-CAC reduction — goals,
      not results.
    </BodyText>
  </SplitFeature>

  <SplitFeature reverse image={{ src: "/cases/crm-copilot/smart-reply.svg", alt: "Placeholder for AI Leasing Bot smart-reply suggestions in the sidebar", width: 1600, height: 900 }}>
    <SectionH small>AI Leasing Bot</SectionH>
    <BodyText>
      An intelligence layer that powers smart-reply suggestions and property
      knowledge inside the copilot: it offers high-quality replies and materials
      an agent can use in one click, and every suggestion shows its data source
      so it can be trusted. I led the copilot-facing suggestion UX. Principles:
      suggestions stay **frictionless and unobtrusive**; trust comes from
      **source attribution** (e.g. “Source: Floor Plan Analysis”); and one click
      to choose, edit, or dismiss feeds a data flywheel. Status: targeted >30%
      adoption of AI-suggested messages within two months — a goal, not a
      measured result.
    </BodyText>
  </SplitFeature>

  <SplitFeature image={{ src: "/cases/crm-copilot/poi.svg", alt: "Placeholder for the POI Mapping Bot response card", width: 1600, height: 900 }}>
    <SectionH small>POI Mapping Bot</SectionH>
    <BodyText>
      A Level-1 knowledge capability of the Leasing Bot: during a prospect chat
      it answers neighborhood questions (“what schools are near this property?”)
      with a short summary, formatted POI cards, and a static map with markers.
      My angle was designing the **response format** — so structured POI data
      (marker, name and address, open status, map and photo preview) renders
      cleanly as copilot cards. It's designing data *for an AI to speak*
      fluently and visually, not just another screen.
    </BodyText>
  </SplitFeature>
